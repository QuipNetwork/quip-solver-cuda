# Experiments log: what we tried while tuning the Otsuka CUDA solver for Aglais (dimer1200)

Author: BudFox. Written 2026-11-19 (day 6 after the spec-120 relaunch).
Build this log belongs to: **Funnel** (our internal name for it was `G3V6L3E`), i.e. the published branch (internally called `all12`; one squashed commit made from 51 local commits, on top of upstream `quip-solver-cuda` at the "solver-core 0.0.15" merge).

The purpose of this file is simple: **so that other miners and maintainers do not have to repeat our experiments.** It lists everything we tried, including the failures and the null results, with sample sizes and an honest grade of how strong each piece of evidence is. It is written for people who already know the Quip / Otsuka solver basics (stage-0 screen, stage-1 short anneal, population annealing + Houdayer, "lanes", leases, qblock rounds) but not our history.

Everything was measured on **one machine with one consumer GPU**, mostly with small samples, and on one problem family. Please read section 8 (limitations) before assuming any number transfers.

---

## 0. Summary: what mattered, in order of measured impact

Ranked by how much the **deep-result rate** (results at or below the winning level per hour) moved, with the evidence grade in brackets (S = strong, M = moderate, W = weak/inconclusive; see section 1).

1. **Anneal length per instance.** Going from ~131k sweeps to 524k sweeps per replica (x4) at 64 replicas was the single biggest step after the solver itself: results at or below the then-winning level went up about 5-10x even though the result rate fell 3-4x. Doubling again (x4096 multiplier) is break-even, x3072 lost. [S for 2048; M for 4096; W for 3072]
2. **Otsuka cluster moves + population annealing (PA) with Houdayer at checkpoints.** About +20 energy units over a tuned single-spin multi-spin-coded SA at equal throughput; without PA the Otsuka kernel was only equal to the single-spin solver. [S]
3. **Softer tile/pair weight (250 instead of 1000).** About 2.2x deep results per hour. [S]
4. **Instance selection (CPU stage-0 screen, then a stage-1 short anneal).** Stage 0 alone ~2-3x; tightening the keep fraction 0.1 -> 0.025 ~1.5-2x more; stage 1 ~1.8x on top (after correcting for noisy per-block estimates). A best-first pool in front of the lanes added about another 1.8x. [M]
5. **`QUIP_OTSUKA_WEAK_SCALE=1.5`** (scale the +-45 weak couplings by 1.5 in the anneal model only; reported energies stay exact; the idea also appears upstream). CPU paired test: -7.8 +- 0.75 units, 44/48 instances deeper. Live: mean 2.9 units deeper in a 30-minute check, deep results per hour roughly doubled. It closed the "weak-layer" gap to the best rival. Do not go above ~1.5. [S offline, M live]
6. **A deep lane** (hold the best first runs of a round and re-anneal them 3-6x longer with a fresh seed, adaptive in number and length): ~15-30x more value per lane-second than ordinary lane work, but only because it works on already-selected candidates, and it only gets ~5-8 % of GPU time. [M]
7. **Kernel engineering (V2..V6 + Shiloach-Vishkin union):** about 1.7x, 1.15x, then ~1.1-1.15x per step in microbenchmarks, all bit-identical; live gains were smaller and noisier than the microbenchmarks predicted. [S microbench, W live]
8. **Things that looked good offline and failed live or were neutral:** beta-knee / "Kcut" schedule, stage-1.5 early abort at a PA checkpoint, V5-as-speedup, V7 bank-conflict permutation, PA frontier Houdayer, faster CPU screen, more screen threads, READS 32, higher WEAK_SCALE, PT/replica-exchange/PT-ICM, every non-Otsuka solver family, re-fitted screen weights, extra screen features, late weak-scale ramp-down, tighter stage-0 keep (0.005), CPU mining in parallel with the GPU.

The honest overall picture: after the first ~two days the remaining ideas were each worth roughly +0-10 % and almost none could be separated from noise in a few-hour on-chain block. The deep-result rate went from ~0.4 / hour (first configuration on this family) to ~10 / hour at or below -5086 units (current configuration), while the winning threshold also moved deeper (about -5079 on day 1 to about -5092 on day 6), mostly because rivals improved.

## 1. Conventions, metrics and evidence grades

* **Energy unit.** "unit" = 1000 milli. All energies are in units, negative, deeper = more negative. Example: -5086.3 is deeper than -5085.0. The dimer1200 family on this chain has a band of -5100 to -4800 units; winning energies were between about -5079 (day 1) and -5092 (day 6).
* **"≤X / h".** Number of full-anneal results per hour whose raw best energy (before CPU polish) is at or below X. The miner logs one record per completed result.
* **"est ≤X / h".** An exponential-tail estimate. Fit the tail slope `lambda = n_u / sum(u - E)` over results at or below a base level `u` (we used -5080.5), then extrapolate `n_u * exp(-lambda * (u - X))`. Per-block lambda is very noisy (a 30-minute block has 8-25 results below the base level, 1-sigma about x/÷2.5 at deep thresholds), so later comparisons used a *pooled* lambda (about 0.65 per unit, i.e. each unit deeper costs a factor of ~1.9 in rate).
* **Rate.** Completed full results per second. Not comparable across configurations that give the lanes different jobs (a deep lane produces few, long results).
* **Evidence grades.**
  * **S**: n of order 50+ paired instances or many hours, consistent direction across replications, or a very large effect.
  * **M**: one replication, or n of order 10-50, or hours of data with overlapping confidence intervals.
  * **W**: a single short block (< 1 hour), n < 10, or confounded.
* **Paired CPU tests.** "CPU paired" means: same real stage-0-passed instances, same seeds, our PA anneal re-implemented on CPU, compared as differences with standard errors. Real instances were taken from live attempts. CPU budgets (16 replicas x 8k-131k sweeps) are far smaller than live (64 x 524k) and CPU absolute energies are 20-40 units shallower than live; **small effects often shrank or vanished when moved to live length** (see knee/Kcut).
* **Days.** `D` is the day of the relaunch with the dimer1200 family (spec 120). `D+1` ... `D+6` are the following days. Times of day are omitted on purpose.
* **Our node** = the miner this log was written on. **Official QPU-backed node** = the Quip team's own annealer-backed node.

## 2. Setup and runtime facts that matter

### 2.1 Hardware / software

* One RTX 5090 desktop GPU (170 SMs), Intel i7-14700KF class CPU (28 threads), WSL2 + Docker on Windows (48 GB RAM given to WSL2, 8 GB swap). The GPU is the only bottleneck: 96-99 % busy, CPU mostly < 75 % used.
* Stock upstream miner image `v0.3.8-rc10`, later `v0.3.9-rc10` (official coordinator with the one-block-earlier submit fix); the solver is our own build of the `quip-cuda-otsuka` binary started through a small launcher script that exports the knobs (section 3.3).
* Everything else (validator, dashboard) stock.
* NVIDIA MPS is a host facility and is not available under WSL2; we never ran it. Our kernel analysis (section 4.2) says the solver is latency/issue-bound at 2 blocks per SM with six lanes already saturating the SMs, so MPS would only matter for sharing the GPU with a second process; our screen is on the CPU, so we did not expect a gain. **Unmeasured.**

### 2.2 Problem and round rules (verified from source and chain data)

* Family: `quip/twisted-ice-dimer`. On day D+1 the chain moved from a 4575-node / 41,474-edge topology (weak 62, pairs 1200) to 4560 nodes / 41,205 edges (weak 45, pairs 1350). **Any offline tool with the old parameters hard-coded generates the wrong instances** (about 33 units too shallow) - read the parameters from the topology metadata.
* Structure of an instance: ~1.77k K4 tiles (strong +-1000 couplings, -2000 each at ice level), 1350 pairs (-1000 each), ~29.2k weak edges at +-45 milli (weak layer is 4.5 % of the strong one). Energy decomposition that we use throughout: **E = -4888 + 2V + W** (units), V = number of violated strong motifs, W = weak-layer energy (about -205 to -213 at winning depth).
* Round (qblock) rules on the runtime we mined:
  * A proof qualifies only if strictly below the threshold of the block that includes it. An earlier block beats a deeper energy; within one block the lowest energy wins; an exact tie goes to the proof applied first; at most 8 proofs per block (about 6 s per block).
  * Start threshold of a round = max(previous winner's energy, previous threshold - 1.000 unit). It is held for 100 blocks (about 10 min) and then eases geometrically toward about -4840 at about 0.12 units per block (about 1.2 units per minute).
  * Typical round length: median ~550 s, q75 ~650 s, q90 ~690 s over ~390 rounds; per-minute hazard of the round ending rises from ~1.5 % in minute 0 to 10-12 % in minutes 6-9 and 60-85 % after minute 10 (ease phase).
  * Roughly a quarter of rounds end by timeout with no winner.
* Instance nonce = BLAKE3(last proof block hash, miner account, salt). It is round-specific and account-specific, so work **cannot be reused across rounds or pooled across accounts**. A result for an old round is useless (the lease sink refuses it).
* Generating one canonical instance costs ~12-15 ms of CPU; the cached generator in our `FAST_GEN` path scores the couplings in ~2.6 ms and only generates canonically for survivors.
* The held-result logic of the stock coordinator is correct: keeps the 8 best per round, submits at once if the result qualifies now, otherwise when the easing threshold reaches it. The stock check is head-based and so lands one block late; the upstream fix (submit the held result one block before the viability block) is in `v0.3.9-rc10`. Lag-0 replays over hundreds of lost rounds flipped almost none (see P04).
* Proof size and priority: a one-row proof is ~2.4 KB, of which ~70 % is the chain-mandated signature. The stock coordinator on this runtime sent all result rows (~20 KB for ~32 rows), tip 0. A patched coordinator that sends only the best row and a small tip made proofs 2.4 KB and fee ~8 % lower, and moved pool priority from 11 to 12. We could not measure a win-rate effect (P03).

### 3.2 What each layer is

* **G3 = the pipeline up to and including deep lane and weak scaling.**
  1. *CPU stage 0* (generate + score with a cheap structural model, 8 threads at ~870 salts/s): model 3 = a cheap model-1 prefilter on all salts, then the expensive model 2 on the lowest 20 %, keeping about 1 % overall. Survivors go into a *best-first pool* (32 entries, 120 s max age); a free lane takes the lowest score first.
  2. *GPU stage 1* (a short anneal of 1/16 of the sweeps on every pool survivor, 2.8 s); a rolling-quantile gate promotes the best 7.5 % to the full run. Screened-out salts are completed with no result unless their short result is already good (SUBMIT_Q 0.5).
  3. *GPU full run*: 64 replicas x 524,288 sweeps, PA with 512 device-side resampling checkpoints, Houdayer at checkpoints, beta 1..12 geometric, tile/pair weight 250, 6 concurrent lanes, **weak couplings scaled x1.5 in the anneal model only**.
  4. *CPU polish* (top-4 replicas: single-spin descent then 1000 Otsuka sweeps at beta 20), 2 threads.
  5. *Stage 2*: a first result in the top 4 % of recent first results is held and re-annealed 6 times with fresh seeds (max 2 groups held), merged, delivered once.
  6. *Deep lane* (adaptive): lanes allocate 0..LMAX holds depending on time in round (none before 120 s, ramping to LMAX by 180 s, one fewer when the round best is already near the target, none after LATE_S), and run the long re-anneal at the largest multiple (3..MULT_MAX) that ends before a ~620 s horizon; reads its PA lineage bests back about once a second and stops and delivers the moment it crosses the target; best candidate first.
* **V6 = class-split gathers + SV union.** The ELL rows are split by coupling magnitude (strong entries first, weak after; multiply once per class per row); Metropolis entries become byte offsets into a shared-memory copy of s and -s; accumulate entries carry the J sign in tagged parents. The union-find pass is replaced by a flat Shiloach-Vishkin-style hook / pointer-jump with a CAS fix-up. Both are **bit-identical** to the V3/V4/V5 results (checked on 96 short and 6 full runs plus 24 other-branch runs on GPU, plus CPU emulation tests).
* **L3 = 3 deep lanes** (`DEEP_LMAX=3`) and `DEEP_LATE_S=600`.
* **E = ease-race booster.** Late in a round (>= 420 s) the round's best result, if it is above the live target but within 1.0 unit, is *held* (instead of sent immediately) and three long runs with fresh, never-repeated seeds are queued ahead of the deep lane's own work; the held result is released when it reaches the target, within a small margin once the ease may have begun, or at the latest around 590 s. Results at or below the target are never held. Reason: the solver core only takes one push per salt, so a delivered salt cannot be improved later.

### 3.4 Features that exist in the code but are OFF (or live on other branches) because they failed or did nothing

In the published branch, off by default and **not recommended**:

* `QUIP_OTSUKA_BETA_KNEE` / `KNEE_FRAC` (beta-knee / "Kcut" schedule): failed live (4.1, A08).
* `QUIP_OTSUKA_FAST` (16-bit parents, 3 blocks/SM): no gain (K01).
* `QUIP_OTSUKA_HOUDAYER_EVERY` > 0: neutral (A11).
* `DEEP_RECORD=0`, `DEEP_SLACK_MILLI`, `DEEP_AFTER_STAGE2`: tried or unneeded (D04).
* `STAGE0_KEEP` below ~0.01 (e.g. 0.005): thinner tail (S06).
* `QUIP_OTSUKA_SWEEP_V5` alone: faster in the microbenchmark, not faster live (K05).
* The older msa-era opt-in knobs (`QUIP_FAMILY_SCREEN`, `QUIP_MSA_*`, `QUIP_BETA_RANGE` for msa) are also in the fork and unused here.

On *separate* experimental branches that were **not** merged into `all12` (so not part of the published build; their source is not included): stage-1.5 early abort (`ABORT_*`, three iterations), V7 bank-conflict permutation, PA frontier Houdayer, faster CPU screen (2.26x/thread), a scheduler ledger and a log-only shadow controller. Each was neutral or negative; details in section 4.

## 5. What decoding on-chain winning proofs taught us (aggregate only)

We decoded on-chain witnesses of winning proofs (about 340 proofs between D+4 and D+6, plus ~250 earlier) against the exact topology; every decoded energy reproduced the chain value exactly. We report nothing per participant on purpose.

* **Everyone lies on the same V-W line, and E is flat in V.** E = -4888 + 2V + W. Among winners the weak-layer energy against V follows W about -202.4 - 1.92 V (residual sd about 1.0) in every group we looked at. A lower V is **not** a deeper energy: removing a violation costs about as much weak energy as it saves (exchange rate -2). Mean E of winners binned by V: V 1-2 -5090.29, V 3 -5090.23, V 4 -5090.21, V 5-8 -5089.82. WEAK_SCALE 1.5 moved our own solutions along this line to a higher-V / lower-W point at equal E (A14). Do not chase V.
* **Solution composition differs, energy does not.** Winning proofs differ in the number of violated *pair* constraints (from about 0.2 to about 2.4 per proof across groups) and in whether the compact "structured" row encoding applies, yet winning energies were not resolvably different. An offline exact repair of pair and tile defects in our own winning states found nothing worth building (A15).
* **The gap is deep-result rate, not winning depth.** Median winning energies were about the same; what differed was the number of deep results per hour. When judging an A/B, count deep results per hour, not round wins.
* **Timing.** Winning times inside a round are spread over minutes 1-12 and the hazard of the round ending rises with time (section 2.2). Rounds are decided by *when* a deep result exists, not only by the best result.
* **An official annealer-backed node** wins in bursts with a low strong-layer defect count. It is not a GPU rival to tune against, and its presence or absence should not decide an A/B.

## 6. Methodology lessons

1. **Selection bias is everywhere in this pipeline.** Every stage conditions on the previous one. Early stage-2 numbers said 36 % of groups deepened; the ledger over 119 groups said a rerun is on average 2.0 units *worse* than its first result (regression to the mean) and only 3.4 % are 2 units better. A short-run to full-run correlation of 0.23 inside the promoted set is not the 0.36-0.6 measured on unselected salts. A "hit rate per run" of a deep lane (18.5 % at ≤-5088) cannot be compared with that of a fresh candidate (~0.5 %): different, pre-selected populations.
2. **Hit rates between lanes are not comparable.** They differ in candidate population, in cost per run (2.8 s / 35 s / 139 s), in how many runs are dropped at a round end (7-31 % of held lane time) and in whether a result is held. The only comparable quantity is *marginal hits per lane-second for a stated candidate*, which requires a ledger with stable candidate ids and an outcome metric - and that is exactly what we did not have until the ledger existed.
3. **Per-block estimates are very noisy.** One block of 30 minutes has ~110 results and 8-25 below the base level of the tail fit; the per-block tail slope moves the deep-threshold estimate by a factor of 2.5 either way. With a pooled lambda (0.65 +- small) the ordering of five "different" stage-1 configurations collapsed into "all the same except stage 1 on versus off". **Use a pooled tail slope and block lengths of 2-4 hours or more.**
4. **Hourly variance is large, even for an unchanged configuration.** 18 consecutive hours: ≤-5086 per hour mean 10.0 (sd 2.6, range 7-17); ≤-5087 5.9 (sd 1.6); ≤-5088 3.4 (sd 1.8, range 1-8); ≤-5089 1.8 (sd 1.5, range 0-5); mean energy per hour -5081.23 +- 0.18. These behave like Poisson counts.
5. **Back-of-envelope power.** To see a +20 % change at about 2 sigma with an unpaired design you need roughly 20 hours per arm if you count ≤-5086 results (10/h), 60 hours per arm at ≤-5088 (3.4/h) and ~50 hours per arm if the process has ~4 events/hour (round wins). A 3-hour block can only separate effects of 60 % or more. Most of our per-block "verdicts" for tweaks smaller than that were noise.
6. **Why win counts cannot be the metric over days.** Wins depend on rivals (who changed their software every few hours), on a threshold that ratchets deeper after every win (so winning more makes the next win harder), on timeouts (~27 % of rounds have no winner), and on bursts of an annealer-backed node. Use deep-result counts at a fixed energy, rescored under old and new yardsticks when the threshold moves.
7. **The baseline-window bug that skewed a baseline.** Our guard/report scripts read only the attempts files modified in the last 900 minutes; as time went on the *start* of a fixed 4-hour baseline block dropped out of the window, silently inflating the new configuration's ratio (reported 1.13-1.17x; the true ratio was 0.90-0.94x, and counted ≤-5086/h 0.73x). Fixed by widening the file-mtime window to 1500 minutes and re-checking; the same trap was in our A/B templates. **Anything that selects data by file modification time is a hazard.**
8. **A moving yardstick.** As rivals pushed the threshold deeper the scoring level moved (-5079 -> -5082.5 -> -5085/-5086); we rescored every item under the old and the new scale and asked before keeping an item if they disagreed. Block scores that used per-block lambda were re-scored with the pooled one.
9. **Microbenchmarks and offline proxies mislead.** (a) A bench against a synthetic ring topology said filling a CUDA block with 256 reads was free; on the real 9x denser graph the same patch lost 15 % attempts/h and 83 % of deep-rate at the deepest level (older era). (b) V5 was +10.6 % in the kernel harness and 0 % live. (c) CPU Kcut at 131k sweeps was neutral, live at 524k it lost depth. (d) CPU effects of ~1-2 units at 8k often shrank to zero at 65k+ (knee). (e) A short-read-count recipe that wins on CPU starves a GPU. **Bench on the real topology at the live length, then A/B live.**
10. **Interleave, and ignore the warm-up.** A/B blocks need a restart at a "new round" line, and the first 8-10 minutes after a restart are not representative (pipeline refill, stash lost). ABBA with 15-minute blocks was too noisy for anything but large effects; 2-4 hour blocks were the minimum that gave stable ordering.
11. **Know what a number is.** The attempt log's qblock id is one lower than the chain's; the public web numbering is one higher than the on-chain counter; energies in the attempt log are raw and pre-polish (polish adds ~0.3-0.55); `lane_wait_frac` can be printed as `e-9`; "lane-seconds" overlap and are not GPU-seconds (lane-busy time summed over lanes is ~6x the union time).
12. **One unproven change at a time, log-only first, one lock for config switches, a safe fallback that is the strongest proven config, a change log with a revert path.** Chain reactions of scripts changing each other's config were the largest operational risk we ran into (P08).
13. **Check configuration equality before comparing.** Before comparing two blocks we compared the sha of binary, wrapper, config, image digest and the full applied-env list (36 variables) - a surprising number of "regressions" turned out to be a different binary or CPU contention (P09).

## 7. What we would try next, and what we would NOT repeat

### 7.1 Try next (ordered by our prior, not by evidence)

1. **Finish H1 (`EASE_GAP_MILLI` 1000 -> 2000)** with a proper interleaved block design, because the booster currently acts in only ~2 % of rounds. If it works, the natural follow-ups are 3000 and a larger `EASE_LANES`; if it does not, drop the booster.
2. **Stage-2 K=3 vs K=6** as a *non-inferiority* test (K=3 saves ~116 lane-s per group; K=6 expected gain 0.57 vs 0.47 units): needs ~100+ groups per arm, i.e. 20+ hours.
3. **Admission and deep-lane allocation by marginal value** (the shadow controller's log is the starting point): the ledger shows the optimum cutoff is flat, so the gain would come from not wasting lane time on holds that will be dropped at round end (7-31 % of held time) and from phase-aware budgeting of stage 1 (47.8 % of lane time).
4. **A stage-0 score calibrated for the deep tail of the *promoted* set** (current within-set Spearman is only 0.15 live): needs unbiased labels, i.e. an occasional unscreened / unfiltered sample paid for in GPU time.
5. **Strong-layer repair that does not trade against W** (all of our attempts moved along the V/W exchange line): a structure-aware move or a targeted post-process on the final state; the evidence that something exists is that one miner reaches the same energy with a different (tile-heavy) violation mix.
6. **Longer anneals at 262k+ for the PT-ICM idea** (the hybrid showed a trend of ~-0.3 per doubling of length on CPU) - only if someone has GPU time to spare.
7. **Kernel:** the profile points at Metropolis (27 % of time, barrier + shared-bank conflicts), accumulate (23 %, long-scoreboard waits on loads of the parent array) and the union (16 %). Realistic remaining headroom is single-digit percent per item; a **persistent kernel** is the only larger structural idea we did not try.
8. **Native Linux / MPS**: unmeasured on this pipeline; we expect only a modest effect now that the screen is CPU-side.

### 7.2 Do NOT repeat (we already paid for these)

* **Beta-knee / "Kcut" schedules** (cut the cold phase): CPU said neutral, live lost depth.
* **Stage-1.5 early abort at a PA checkpoint** (even though the correlation is 0.968): freed time was not turned into deep results.
* **`WEAK_SCALE` above 1.5**, or a late ramp-down to 1.0: the exchange rate between V and W eats it.
* **Tile/pair weight other than ~250** under WEAK_SCALE 1.5; **`beta_min` 2**; larger `bmax`.
* **`SWEEP_MULT` 4096 or 3072**, **`READS` 32** (GPU starvation), larger `CONCURRENCY`, 3 blocks per SM.
* **PT / replica exchange / PT-ICM** at current lengths; **single-spin SA/Gibbs/msa/SB** families; the structure-aware CPU solvers of the image as stand-alone solvers; warm restarts instead of fresh seeds for re-annealing.
* **Re-fitting the stage-0 weights**, the upstream 4-feature screen on top of model 2, spectral/structural/greedy features, short CPU anneals as extra labels.
* **`STAGE0_KEEP` 0.005**, 12 screen threads, the faster CPU screen with fewer threads: the pool is already saturated.
* **Trusting a kernel microbenchmark as a speed-up** without a live A/B on the real topology (V5, min-reads patch).
* **Running offline analysis jobs on the miner's host during an A/B block** (we contaminated two blocks).
* **Per-block lambda scoring**, hourly win counts, and baseline windows selected by file mtime.
* **Parallel CPU mining next to the GPU**: -32 % GPU attempts/hour in the one test we ran.

## 8. Honest limitations

* **One machine, one GPU, one operator.** A single RTX 5090 desktop on WSL2 + Docker Desktop with a 28-thread consumer CPU. Everything GPU-side (lane count saturating at 6, 2 blocks/SM, power ~450-470 W, concurrency effects, CPU contention) may be different on other cards, on native Linux, or on multi-GPU hosts.
* **Small samples.** Most live A/B blocks are 20 minutes to 4 hours with 100-1000 results; deep-tail counts at the thresholds that matter are single digits per hour. Where we quote CIs they are mostly bootstrap or Poisson-based and wide. Many verdicts are "no detectable difference", not "no difference".
* **CPU-to-live transfer is imperfect.** CPU harness budgets (16 replicas x 8k-131k sweeps) are much smaller than live (64 x 524k); CPU energies are 20-40 units shallower; at least one effect (knee/Kcut) failed to transfer.
* **One problem family and one runtime.** All of this is for `quip/twisted-ice-dimer` with weak 45 / pairs 1350 (earlier 62 / 1200) on one chain, days D to D+6. Different families, different parameters or a runtime change can invalidate screen models, WEAK_SCALE, tile/pair weights and the deep-lane timing (which is tuned to ~9-minute rounds and a 100-block hold).
* **The rivals moved.** Thresholds drifted ~13 units deeper in six days; rival software changed during our experiments; any comparison across hours confounds our change with the competition.
* **Attribution is partial.** Several improvements were deployed together (e.g. V6 + L3 + upgrade to the official 0.3.9-rc10 within a few hours), so the live gain of V6 is not cleanly separated from the others; the ease booster fired once in a 4-hour block, so its only evidence is non-inferiority.
* **Ledger/shadow findings come from ~3.7 hours of data** (28 complete rounds) and describe where time goes, not what would have happened under another policy.
* **The rebase onto current upstream is not re-verified.** The build is `all12` on top of upstream at the solver-core 0.0.15 merge; upstream may have moved. The kernel changes are bit-identical to what they replace *on our checks* (96 short + 6 full runs on GPU plus CPU emulation); other GPUs/drivers/NVRTC versions and other families (V6 requires <= 2 coupling magnitudes and <= 8 strong entries per row, otherwise it falls back to V5's layout) have not been checked. The CPU test suites pass (450 lib + 43 session tests for `all12`), but the GPU-only paths (deep-lane probe, V6, SV union, ease booster delivery) can only be tested on a GPU.
* **No security review** of the patched coordinator or of the scripts we used around the miner; do not treat them as upstream-quality.
* **H1 has no verdict**; anything in this file about the ease booster beyond "non-inferior, rarely active" is a hypothesis.

### A.2 The earliest epoch: single-spin SA miner (Python kernel, then early Rust miner)

| "Garbage" energies | Root cause: the flip-energy accumulator and `delta_energy` workspace were `signed char`; the hot-loop update `delta_energy[neighbor] += multiplier * Jij * ns` (up to +-508) overflows int8 on every flip, so SA accepts energy-increasing flips on high-degree blocks (instance-dependent).
